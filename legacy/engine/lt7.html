What Makes the LT7 engineSpecial

The LT7's flat-plane crankshaft is the defining characteristic that separates it from every previous Corvette engine. A flat-plane crank fires alternating banks at 180° intervals — like two four-cylinder engines sharing a crankshaft. This allows superior exhaust scavenging between cylinders and enables a much higher natural revving ceiling.

Pairing a flat-plane crank with twin turbochargers required extensive engineering — particularly the exhaust manifold routing. Each bank's exhaust feeds its own turbo, maintaining the pulse separation that flat-plane engines depend on for efficiency. The result is instant turbo response and a howling exhaust note unlike any other turbocharged V8.

The LT7 enginealso inherits the sodium-hollow exhaust valves from the Z06's LT6 engine, the LT7's naturally-aspirated sibling. Sodium inside the hollow valve stem conducts heat away from the valve head — critical near the 8,100 rpm sixth-gear redline under boost.

Peak Efficiency & Performance

For the ZR1 and ZR1X LT7, the peak range depends on whether you are looking at torque, horsepower, cruising efficiency, or absolute top speed.

The LT7 dyno plot for the 2025, 2026, and 2027 engine shows horsepower and torque versus RPM. Source: Chevrolet.com.

Peak torque band. The torque curve is essentially flat between 3,000 and 6,750 RPM, consistently delivering over 800 lb-ft. Peak torque is 828 lb-ft at 6,000 RPM. Torque is the force the engine puts on the crankshaft, and you feel it as the push that puts you into the seat.

Peak power band. The engine reaches its maximum 1,064 horsepower at 7,000 RPM. Power is how quickly that torque is delivered to the wheels, and it is what you feel as the car keeps accelerating.

Top-speed gear. To hit the 233 mph top speed, the sweet spot is sixth gear. GM uses an 8,100 RPM redline only in sixth gear so the car can keep accelerating. The power band drops off too steeply in seventh and eighth to hold that speed. You can still cruise the ZR1 or ZR1X at 60 mph in eighth gear at about 1,400 RPM without the engine bogging down or collecting carbon.

Valvetrain & Injection

DOHC Finger-Follower Valvetrain

The LT7 engineuses a roller finger-follower valvetrain — the same architecture found in high-revving European performance engines. Compared to traditional pushrod rocker arms, finger followers reduce friction and reciprocating mass, enabling the LT7 engineto rev cleanly to the 8,100 rpm sixth-gear redline despite producing enormous low-end torque from the turbos.

Hydraulic lash adjusters maintain valve clearances automatically, eliminating the manual valve adjustments required by solid-lifter designs.

Dual-Port Fuel Injection

The LT7 enginecombines both direct injection (DI) and port fuel injection (PFI) in a single system. DI injects fuel directly into the combustion chamber at high pressure, ideal for power and efficiency under load. PFI injects into the intake port, keeping intake valves clean from carbon buildup — a well-known issue with DI-only engines.

The ECU automatically blends both systems based on RPM, load, and temperature, maximizing combustion efficiency across the entire operating range.
