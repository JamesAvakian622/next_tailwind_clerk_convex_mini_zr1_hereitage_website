Turbos, Exhaust & Cooling

The largest turbos ever fitted to a production Corvette — paired with a uniquely routed exhaust system, heat exchangers, and a dual-port injection system that feeds 1,064 HP.

Why Twin Turbos Work Here

The flat-plane crankshaft of the LT7 enginecreates a natural firing order that keeps exhaust pulses from each bank separated — exactly what twin-scroll turbos need to spool quickly and efficiently. Each bank's four cylinders feed their own turbocharger, maintaining pulse energy without cross-bank interference.

The "hot-vee" placement puts the turbos in the valley between the cylinder banks. This shortens exhaust runners dramatically, reducing turbo lag. The trade-off is intense heat management — which GM solves with water-to-air intercoolers and multiple heat exchangers in the front fascia.

The result: essentially no perceivable turbo lag at any RPM, a linear powerband from idle to 8,400 rpm, and a sound that blends a flat-plane V8's high-pitched scream with the whoosh of compressed air.

Exhaust System

Active Exhaust & Sound

The LT7's exhaust exits through electronically-controlled active valves that open fully in Sport and Track modes. In Tour mode, the valves partially close — reducing cabin drone and meeting drive-by noise regulations.

The flat-plane crank's firing order — combined with individual-bank turbocharging — produces a distinct high-frequency exhaust note that's unlike any previous Corvette. The LT7 enginescreams rather than rumbles, with a sharp crack on upshifts in full-throttle mode.

Quad exhaust outlets exit at the rear center of the vehicle — a signature visual element of the C8 ZR1's tail. The titanium-coated exhaust tips are standard on all trim levels.

Heat Exchangers & Cooling

Intercooling

Water-to-Air Intercoolers

Two water-to-air intercoolers cool the compressed charge air from each turbo before it enters the intake plenum — critical for maintaining density and preventing knock.

Radiators

Front Heat Exchangers

The ZR1's front fascia houses multiple heat exchangers — engine coolant, transmission fluid, intercooler water circuits, and power steering fluid all require cooling at 1,064 HP output levels.

Lubrication

Dry-Sump Oiling

An 8-stage dry-sump scavenging system ensures consistent oil pressure under extreme cornering G-forces and during full-throttle track sessions where a wet sump would starve.
