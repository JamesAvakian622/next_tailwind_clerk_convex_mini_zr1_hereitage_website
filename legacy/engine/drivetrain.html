How the DCT Works

A dual-clutch transmission uses two separate clutch packs — one for odd gears (1, 3, 5, 7) and one for even gears (2, 4, 6, 8). While driving in 1st gear, the transmission pre-selects 2nd gear on the second clutch. When the shift signal comes, one clutch opens and the other closes simultaneously — with zero torque interruption.

The Tremec unit is positioned as a rear transaxle — the differential and transmission share a single housing at the rear axle. This placement (necessary for the mid-engine layout) also improves front/rear weight balance. On the ZR1, the result is a near-perfect 40/60 front/rear weight distribution.

In manual mode, paddle shifters give the driver direct control. In automatic Track mode, the transmission holds gears aggressively to keep the LT7 engineon the top of its powerband.

Electronic Limited-Slip Differential

eLSD — Torque Vectoring Rear Axle

The electronic limited-slip differential (eLSD) can bias torque between the rear wheels in milliseconds. Under hard acceleration, it locks up to send maximum torque to the wheel with the most traction. In cornering, it can apply a small amount of diff lock to the outside rear wheel, effectively yawing the car into the corner.

The eLSD works in concert with the PTM system — both systems share sensor inputs from wheel speed sensors, accelerometers, and steering angle. Together they make the ZR1 exploitable and adjustable, not just electronically nannied.

E-AWD System (ZR1X Only)

System type Electric front axle + ICE rear axle
Front drive Electric motor driving front axle
Rear drive LT7 engine + 8-speed DCT transaxle
Torque split Variable front/rear, software-controlled
Stealth mode Front eMotor only — silent operation
Motor blending Seamless LT7 engine + eMotor torque fill
Battery High-voltage hybrid pack
Regen braking Front axle regenerative braking

The ZR1X's E-AWD system adds a front electric motor to the base ZR1's rear-drive DCT drivetrain. The front motor operates independently of the LT7 engine— it can run alone in Stealth Mode, fill torque gaps during shifts, or combine with the LT7 enginefor maximum acceleration.

Motor blending is handled by the ZR1X's ECU, which monitors throttle demand, wheel slip, and battery state of charge to decide how much torque each axle receives. Under hard launch from a standstill, both axles fire simultaneously — explaining the ZR1X's sub-2.5 second 0–60 capability.
